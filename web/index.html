How lineups are calculated

Calculating unit probabilities and acquisition costs

Let p denote the probability that one shop slot contains the target unit. We estimate it as follows:

p = a × r / R

Here, a is the probability of rolling the target cost tier at the current level, r is the number of remaining copies of the target unit, and R is the total number of remaining copies in that tier. For Lux, the model assumes that all available forms are equally likely, so this probability is also divided by the number of forms.

We then calculate the expected number of shops required to find this unit on its own:

q = 1 − (1 − p)5d = 1 / q

Here, q is the probability that a shop contains at least one copy of the target unit, and d is the expected number of shops needed to find it. A unit that cannot appear in the shop has an infinite acquisition cost. Owned units have an acquisition cost of 0.

Carrying acquired units across levels, crowns, and evolutions

Calculations advance through player levels, crown counts, and evolution counts. For the state (L, C, E), the predecessor states are:

(L − 1, C, E)(L, C − 1, E)(L, C, E − 1)

Here, L is the player level, C is the number of crowns, and E is the number of Kha'Zix evolutions. Only predecessor states with valid coordinates are used.

Each predecessor passes on its acquired pool and its selected lineup. Without a click, the first lineup in Loss ↑ is selected. Across trait counts, equal losses favor more traits. Within the same trait count, equal-cost lineups favor actually owned units; inherited units do not count as owned. These acquired units cost 0 in successors. Changing the sort is not a selection. The game pool still displays all candidate units.

When a unit is marked as no longer owned, its historical acquisition records are cleared and its acquisition cost is recalculated. The records for other units are retained.

Finding the lowest acquisition cost for each trait count

For each attainable trait count, integer programming finds a lineup with the smallest sum of acquisition costs, subject to population limits and trait activation rules. Equal-cost solutions favor owned units.

If both criteria are equal, a fixed order of unit IDs and evolution classes determines the lineup.

Kha'Zix's evolution classes are optimized together with the lineup. Confirmed evolutions are retained.

Comparing states with a shared loss and threshold

Loss is the sum of the lineup’s additional acquisition costs:

D = ∑ di

Loss D is the sum of the expected shop waits for each missing unit, measured in shops. It is 0 when no units are missing.

The shared threshold uses a fixed reference set. Grouping log scores V = 100/(1+D) minimizes the within-group squared deviations. The boundary is then converted to a loss threshold of —. Loss above it appears in red and its lineup row is dimmed.

The model assumes that the pool remains unchanged while searching and that the five shop slots are independent.

Rules

Each emblem adds 1 to its corresponding trait count across the entire lineup.

Set Kha'Zix's evolution count to calculate a lineup and evolution classes together. “Confirm evolutions” saves those classes for this game. Clicking a confirmed trait icon undoes that evolution and reduces the evolution count by 1. “Reset” clears the game's records.

Lux contributes 2 to the trait of her selected form. Elder Dragon occupies 2 population slots and contributes 2 to Riftbeast.

The calculator uses current shop probabilities to calculate lineup loss D, measured in shops. Lower loss means missing units are easier to find. Loss above the shared loss threshold appears in red and its lineup row is dimmed.

In lineups, the session pool, and the ownership picker, colored portraits represent owned units and gray portraits represent units you do not own. In the ban list, colored portraits represent allowed units and gray portraits represent excluded units.

Each lineup solve has an 8-second limit. “✓ Proven optimal” means HiGHS has proved both the minimum cost and tie-breaking order optimal under the current conditions. “◷ Optimality unproven” means a feasible lineup is available, but the full proof is incomplete.
